# SSCIM public guide

## A simple way to use SSCIM

1. Pick a reported event or build a scenario.
2. Read the event source, date, confidence, and stated affected stages.
3. Follow the selected item across the map, supply-chain graph, and topology.
4. Treat a high score as a question to investigate, not a prediction.

## What the views mean

The **map** aggregates modeled stage exposure by country. The **industry flow** shows how the model connects production functions. The **topology** expands this into functional centres such as `Japan × photoresists` or `Taiwan × advanced fab`.

Topology links are modeled stage-mediated relationships. They are not shipping lanes, measured trade flows, or proof that two companies transact directly.

## How to read a score

- **Structural vulnerability** is a relatively stable model property: concentration, network influence, policy exposure, substitutability, and market sensitivity.
- **Operational impact** is the model’s response to the selected event or scenario.
- **Contribution** is a share of an aggregate modeled effect, not a financial-loss estimate.

Scores help compare items inside this model. They do not prove causation or predict market prices.

## Before sharing a conclusion

Check the event source, confidence, event age, stated assumptions, and the model limitations. Use primary reporting for consequential claims. SSCIM is a research aid, not investment, legal, or operational advice.
